A lot of crews show up in League City expecting uniform clay and end up punching through a sand lens they never saw coming. The Clear Creek watershed carved a complicated subsurface over thousands of years — old channel deposits, oxbow fills, and discontinuous sand stringers hide beneath the gumbo clay. We run vertical electrical sounding to map those contrasts before the backhoe ever hits the ground. Resistivity shifts tell you where the clay transitions to sand, where the water table actually sits, and whether that "dry" pad is sitting on a perched zone. For sites near the Dickinson Bayou or along the FM 518 corridor, we often combine VES profiles with MASW surveys to tie the stratigraphy to shear-wave velocity, and CPT soundings when we need a direct push confirmation through the Beaumont Formation clays.

In the Beaumont Formation clays of League City, a 5 ohm-m drop across a transect often means the difference between stable fat clay and a water-charged sand lens.

Methodology and scope

ASTM D6431 sets the standard for resistivity imaging, and here in League City it matters more than in drier parts of Texas. The upper 15 to 30 feet are dominated by Pleistocene Beaumont Formation deposits — stiff to hard fat clays interbedded with silts and fine sands. Resistivity values swing from below 8 ohm-m in saturated, saline clays to over 80 ohm-m in clean sand lenses. That contrast is what lets a Schlumberger array pick out paleochannels. We set the electrode spread to reach depths of 60 to 100 feet, enough to see beneath typical commercial pad footprints. On larger tracts east of I-45 — think the Victory Lakes area — we run parallel transects to build a quasi-3D picture, flagging zones where differential settlement could bite later. The data feeds directly into the geotechnical model, reducing the number of borings needed and giving the structural engineer a continuous profile instead of point data from SPT drilling alone.

Local considerations

What catches people off guard in League City isn't just the sand — it's the perched water sitting on top of it. We've scanned sites off Calder Road where resistivity dropped sharply at 8 feet, right where a thin silt layer trapped groundwater above the main table. Nobody spotted it during the preliminary site walk because the surface was dry. That kind of missed detail leads to excavation blowouts, shoring failures, or slab moisture problems down the line. The sulfate-rich clays common in the area also accelerate corrosion on buried utilities; when resistivity dips below 10 ohm-m, you know the soil is aggressive. Overlooking this means concrete degradation and pipe pitting in less than a decade. A well-run VES survey maps those corrosive zones before you pour, and gives the civil team a basis to specify Type V cement or protective coatings where they're actually needed — not just everywhere as a blanket cost add-on.

Associated technical services

01

VES for Foundation & Pavement Design

We run Schlumberger soundings at boring locations to extend the soil profile laterally, giving you a corridor view instead of isolated logs. This helps position spread footings and mat foundations where clay thickness is consistent, avoiding abrupt transitions. We also flag high-resistivity sand pockets that can cause differential movement under repeated truck loading in warehouse slabs.

02

ERT Transects for Site-Wide Characterization

For tracts over two acres — common in the Tuscan Lakes and Hidden Lakes developments — we lay out multi-electrode Wenner arrays to produce continuous 2D resistivity sections. The output integrates with your CAD base map and highlights buried channels, fill zones, and areas of elevated soil corrosivity. This data often satisfies the City of League City's geotechnical review requirements when paired with targeted borings.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a resistivity survey cost for a typical League City commercial lot?

For a standard commercial lot — say one to three acres with a single VES spread or a short ERT line — the work typically runs between US$720 and US$1,050. The exact figure depends on how many soundings or linear feet of transect you need, plus the depth of investigation. We can give you a firm number after reviewing the site plan and the geotechnical scope.

What does resistivity tell me that a boring doesn't?

A boring gives you a discrete point at one location. Resistivity fills in the gaps between borings, showing how clay and sand layers connect laterally. In League City, where old Clear Creek channels create sharp stratigraphic changes over short distances, that continuous profile is what lets you place footings with confidence instead of guessing what's 30 feet away from the nearest log.

Can you distinguish fat clay from lean clay with resistivity?

Not directly — both are fine-grained and their resistivity overlap depends more on pore water salinity and saturation than on plasticity. But we can distinguish clay-dominated sections from silt and sand interbeds very reliably. When you combine the resistivity profile with Atterberg limit testing from a few calibration borings, the picture sharpens considerably and you can map the fat clay zones across the site.
